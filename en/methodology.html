How do we count?

Last updated: 8 November 2026

Mastaba (المصطبة) is a weekly chart of Arabic podcasts on YouTube. These are all the rules we rank by, with no secrets. If a rule changes, we'll write here what changed and when, before we apply it.

The idea in one line

Every week we count the views each podcast's episodes gained on YouTube during that week, and rank the podcasts by that. It doesn't matter who is biggest. What matters is who was watched most this week.

Where do the numbers come from?

Only from YouTube's public numbers: the view, like and comment counts anyone can see under a video. We read them every day through YouTube's official API. We don't use any private data, and we never ask anyone to sign in.

YouTube only shows you today's number, so we record it every day to work out the week. We follow more than 400 podcasts.

The ranking is built on views. Likes and comments are used only in the quality check (see below).

The chart week

  • The week starts on Friday and ends the following Friday.
  • We take a reading of every episode on Friday morning, and another the next Friday. The difference between them is the week's views.
  • The chart is published on the Sunday after.

Who gets on the chart?

A podcast has to be:

  • A conversation between two or more people, with a consistent host. Lectures, monologues, readings and narration don't qualify.
  • Published on YouTube, with full episodes.
  • At least 3 full episodes in the last 90 days, so the show is still running.

A podcast that publishes its full episodes only on audio platforms won't appear here, and that's deliberate: we count YouTube only, and we say so plainly.

What counts?

  • Full episodes only: 20 minutes or more.
  • Episodes published in the last 90 days.
  • An episode published mid-week counts from zero.
  • A live stream counts if it was a conversation episode of the podcast and it has ended.

An episode older than 90 days drops out of the count, so a show can fall in the ranking even if its numbers didn't go down.

What doesn't count?

  • Shorts, clips and any video under 20 minutes.
  • Re-uploads: if the same episode was uploaded twice, it counts only once. And if another channel uploaded a copy of an episode, only the original counts.
  • Compilations such as "best moments" and "highlights".
  • Episodes whose engagement is far below normal (fewer than 1 comment per 10,000 views and fewer than 5 likes per 1,000) are not counted, since that usually means views from paid ads.
  • Videos that aren't episodes, such as a live stream of a match, or vlogs.

Channels with more than one podcast

If one channel publishes several podcasts, each podcast counts on its own and gets its own place. The channel itself is not on the chart.

The quality check

Sometimes an episode gets far more views than the podcast's normal level, while its comments are far below the usual. That usually means views coming from paid ads, not from an audience that engages.

In that case we don't remove the episode, and we don't accuse anyone of anything. We count its views only up to a reasonable ceiling compared with the podcast's normal level, and the excess above it isn't counted. Episodes that genuinely take off show the engagement to match, so they aren't affected.

New podcasts on المصطبة

Any podcast we add joins the chart from the first full week we've followed it from start to finish, so the count is fair.

The chart's sections

Top 20: the 20 podcasts that gained the most views this week.

Rising: the podcasts whose week was far above their own average over the last 4 weeks. Who is growing, not who is biggest. So that the board introduces new names, shows in this week's Top 20 don't enter it. And a show's average over the past four weeks must be at least five thousand views a week, so that a very small show can't top the board with a jump from small numbers.

Episode of the Week: the episode with the most views in its first 7 days, among the episodes published the week before.

Money and the ranking

No one can pay to appear on the chart or to change their place. Ever. If one day we have sponsors or ads, that will never touch the ranking.

What we don't claim

المصطبة doesn't say who is "most listened to" or "most popular". We measure one clear thing: public YouTube views in one week. A podcast may have a large audience on Spotify or Apple Podcasts, and that's something we don't see.

If you find a mistake

We can make mistakes. If an episode was counted wrongly, or a podcast was put in the wrong place, write to us at hello@mastaba.fm. If the mistake is confirmed, we'll fix it and say so publicly.
